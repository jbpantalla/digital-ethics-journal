    SECTION 2 | MORAL MOMENT 1 OF 6

    Choosing the Team Over Sleep

    TOPIC Group project and lost sleep

    ETHICAL LENS
    • UTILITARIANISM
    • TEAMWORK

    THE SITUATION

    Our group was working on our iBreaker Capstone Project and preparing to set it up as a website. There were still important tasks to complete, so I used my free time to help the group instead of resting.

    THE ETHICAL CONFLICT

    I had to choose between protecting my sleep and helping my group finish the project. If I rested, I would feel better, but the team might struggle to complete the website on time.

    ETHICAL LENS

    Utilitarianism judges an action by its consequences. The right choice is the one that produces the greatest overall good, counting the well-being of everyone affected, including myself. In this situation, my lost rest was a real cost, but it affected only me. Helping the group meant the whole team could complete and submit the website properly. The benefit to many outweighed the cost to one.

    Theory link: Utilitarianism fits because the benefit to the whole group was greater than the cost of my lost rest.

    MY DECISION

    I decided to help my group with the iBreaker website. I believed that my sacrifice would benefit more people, the whole group, than keeping my free time for myself.

    MY MORAL INSIGHT

    I learned that ethical decisions sometimes require personal sacrifice, and helping others can be the better choice when it brings a greater benefit to everyone involved. I also learned that I count too. If I neglect my health, I may be less able to help anyone later, so I should manage my time and plan ahead.

    SECTION 2 | MORAL MOMENT 2 OF 6

    Honest When No One Is Watching

    TOPIC A found wristwatch

    ETHICAL LENS
    • DEONTOLOGY
    • HONESTY

    THE SITUATION

    I found my classmate’s wristwatch on the bathroom sink. Nobody was watching, so I could have kept it without anyone knowing.

    THE ETHICAL CONFLICT

    At first, I thought keeping the watch might be easy because nobody would find out. However, I knew it belonged to my classmate and that they might be worried about losing it. I had to choose between taking the easy path and doing what was honest and responsible.

    ETHICAL LENS

    Deontology says an action is right when it follows moral duty, not because of its results. Immanuel Kant taught that we should act only on rules that everyone could follow, which he called the categorical imperative. If everyone kept things they found that belonged to others, no one’s belongings would be safe, so keeping the watch cannot be a rule for everyone. Kant also taught that we must respect others as persons. Keeping my classmate’s watch would have ignored their worry and their right to their own property. For Kant, doing the right thing matters most when it is done out of duty, even when nobody would know.

    Theory link: Deontology fits because I returned the watch out of duty, not because of the results or because anyone might find out.

    MY DECISION

    I gave the watch back to my classmate. I did it because I knew it was the honest and responsible thing to do, not because anyone was watching.

    MY MORAL INSIGHT

    I learned that honesty is tested most when no one is watching. Doing the right thing means following my sense of duty, not only worrying about whether I might be caught.

    SECTION 2 | MORAL MOMENT 3 OF 6

    Calm Before Blame

    TOPIC A mistake in our capstone website

    ETHICAL LENS
    • VIRTUE ETHICS
    • PATIENCE

    THE SITUATION

    During our capstone project, I noticed a mistake in our website work. I told my group about it calmly.

    THE ETHICAL CONFLICT

    When I noticed the mistake, I felt a little upset because it could have caused more work and delayed the project. I could have reacted with anger or blamed someone, but I knew that would not fix the problem and could hurt my group’s teamwork. I had to decide whether to follow my frustration or respond with patience.

    ETHICAL LENS

    Aristotle’s virtue ethics asks what kind of person I should become. He taught that virtues are developed through practice and habit, and that each virtue is a balance between two extremes. In this case, the virtue is good temper, or patience, which sits between being hot-tempered and being too passive to speak up at all. A person with good character feels the right amount of anger, at the right time, toward the right matter, and in the right way. I did feel upset, but virtue does not mean having no feelings. It means managing them wisely. Practical wisdom helped me choose to speak up calmly and work on a solution instead of pointing fingers.

    Theory link: Aristotle’s virtue ethics fits because I practiced patience as a habit of character, balancing anger and silence.

    MY DECISION

    I controlled my temper and discussed the mistake calmly with my group. I used patience and self-control to help fix the problem together.

    MY MORAL INSIGHT

    I learned that how I respond to a mistake matters as much as finding it. Staying calm and avoiding blame keeps the team working together, and each time I choose patience, it becomes more of a habit and part of my character.

    SECTION 2 | MORAL MOMENT 4 OF 6

    Notes Without a Price Tag

    TOPIC Sharing notes with a classmate

    ETHICAL LENS
    • CHRISTIAN ETHICS
    • KINDNESS

    THE SITUATION

    A classmate missed class, so I shared my notes and helped them understand the lesson.

    THE ETHICAL CONFLICT

    I was also busy with my own schoolwork and had limited time. I could have focused only on my own responsibilities, but I knew my classmate needed support. I had to choose between protecting my time and being generous to someone who could not repay me.

    ETHICAL LENS

    Christian ethics centers on loving God and loving one’s neighbor as oneself. It values kindness, compassion, and service that does not expect anything in return. Teachings such as the Golden Rule and the parable of the Good Samaritan call believers to help anyone in need, not only friends. By sharing my notes and explaining the lesson, I was treating my classmate as a neighbor who deserved care, and my help was a gift, not a trade.

    Theory link: Christian ethics fits because I helped a neighbor in need without expecting anything back.

    MY DECISION

    I shared my notes and helped my classmate understand the lesson. I did not expect anything in return, because my values taught me to be kind and help someone who needed support. I tried to balance helping them with finishing my own responsibilities.

    MY MORAL INSIGHT

    I learned that small acts of kindness are a real way of living out my values. Helping others does not need a reward, and sharing what I know can make a real difference to someone who is struggling. I also learned that I can be generous while still taking care of my own duties, as long as I balance both.

    SECTION 2 | MORAL MOMENT 5 OF 6

    Small Chores, Big Harmony

    TOPIC Helping at home while tired

    ETHICAL LENS
    • EASTERN VIRTUE ETHICS
    • FAMILY

    THE SITUATION

    I was tired and wanted to rest, but I helped my parents with household chores.

    THE ETHICAL CONFLICT

    I had to choose between resting and doing my share at home. Resting would have felt better in that moment, but my parents needed help, and I knew that family responsibilities should be shared. I also had to decide whether to complain or to help quietly.

    ETHICAL LENS

    Eastern virtue ethics, especially the Confucian tradition, teaches that good character is built through everyday relationships, beginning with the family. It values respect for parents, known as filial piety, along with benevolence, proper conduct, and harmony with others. In this situation, helping my parents without complaining was a way to practice respect and care. It also protected harmony at home, which Confucian thought treats as a sign of good relationships.

    Theory link: Eastern virtue ethics fits because I practiced respect for my parents and kept harmony at home through a small, everyday action.

    MY DECISION

    I helped my parents with the household chores even though I was tired. I did not complain, because I respect them and I wanted to keep harmony at home.

    MY MORAL INSIGHT

    I learned that virtue is built through small, everyday actions, not only big moments. Respecting my family and sharing responsibilities, even when I am tired, helps me become a better person and keeps our home peaceful.

    Sharing the Load Fairly

    TOPIC Dividing the capstone workload

    ETHICAL LENS
    • DISTRIBUTIVE JUSTICE
    • FAIRNESS

    THE SITUATION

    While working on our iBreaker Capstone Project, our group had to divide the work for setting up the website.

    THE ETHICAL CONFLICT

    We had to decide how to share the work in a way that was fair to everyone. Splitting everything equally might ignore that members had different skills and available time, but giving one person too much would be unfair to that person. The question was what a fair share really meant.

    ETHICAL LENS

    Distributive justice is about how benefits and burdens, such as work, are shared fairly in a group. A fair division can be based on equality, need, effort, or contribution. Aristotle taught that fairness means treating people in proportion to relevant differences, not always identically. John Rawls added that a fair arrangement is one we would accept without knowing which share we would receive. In our group, assigning tasks by skills, available time, and contribution was a fair way to share the workload, because it recognized each member’s situation and prevented one person from carrying everything.

    Theory link: Distributive justice fits because we divided the burden by skills, time, and contribution so that no one carried everything.

    MY DECISION

    We assigned tasks fairly according to each member’s skills, available time, and contribution, so that no one person carried the entire workload.

    MY MORAL INSIGHT

    I learned that fairness does not always mean identical shares. It means considering each person’s abilities and circumstances so the burden is shared in a reasonable way. A fair workload keeps the team motivated and helps the project succeed.

    SECTION 3 | MY GROWTH

    The Moral Person I Am Becoming

    MY ETHICAL THINKING

    Before I studied ethics, I usually decided what to do based on what felt right, what was convenient, or what would help me avoid problems. Now I understand that right and wrong can be examined in different ways. I think more carefully about the consequences of my actions, my duties, my character, my faith, my relationships with others, and whether people are being treated fairly. Writing my six journal entries showed me that ethical choices are not only found in big moments. They are also in small, everyday situations like helping a group, returning something I found, or staying calm when a problem appears.

    Deontology influenced me the most. It taught me that doing the right thing should not depend on whether someone is watching or whether I will benefit. When I found my classmate’s wristwatch, I returned it because honesty and respect were my duty, even though keeping it would have been easier. That experience helped me see that real integrity is tested when no one is checking.

    MY VALUES

    Through this project, I strengthened several values. I practiced honesty by returning the wristwatch. I practiced responsibility by contributing my time to our iBreaker Capstone Project. I practiced patience and self-control by staying calm when problems came up in our work. I practiced fairness by helping divide the group’s workload according to each member’s abilities and contribution.

    MY ETHICAL GROWTH

    In my personal life, I will practice honesty, respect, patience, and responsibility with my family, friends, and classmates. I will help others when they need support, but I will also learn to balance their needs with my own health and responsibilities.

    In my future profession as a computer engineer, I will be honest in my work and fair when working with a team. I will be responsible when handling technology and people’s information. I will stay patient when solving technical problems, and I will make ethical decisions even when no one is checking.

    MY COMMITMENT

    As a moral person, I commit myself to being honest even when no one is watching, to treating others with respect and fairness, to staying patient and self-controlled when things get difficult, and to using my skills to help others while taking responsibility for my own actions.
